Thermodynamic Modeling (CALPHAD)

High-performance Cu–Ni–Si "Corson" alloys rely on nanoscale intermetallic precipitates (δ–Ni₂Si) to achieve an optimal balance of mechanical strength and electrical conductivity. To map this process, I developed a digital twin utilizing the CALPHAD (Calculation of Phase Diagrams) approach. Using the pycalphad library and a custom ternary thermodynamic database (TDB), an 800°C isothermal section was computed to define the equilibrium solubility boundaries for the FCC_A1 matrix and the strengthening δ-phase.

Kinetic Simulation of Microstructural Evolution

While thermodynamic equilibrium establishes the boundaries, real-world precipitation is governed by time-dependent kinetics. A comprehensive MATLAB model was written to simulate isothermal aging at 450°C over 4 hours. The kinetic solver integrates:

  • Classical Nucleation Theory (CNT): Computes the time-dependent nucleation rate of new particles based on the chemical driving force derived from the CALPHAD solubility product.
  • Zener-Hillert Growth Model: Solves diffusion-limited particle growth, accounting for instantaneous matrix solute depletion via iterative mass balance equations.

Simulation Results & Validation

The integrated model captures the complete temporal evolution of the microstructure. Over the 4-hour aging cycle, the simulation tracks a rapid spike in precipitate number density, followed by saturation as growth dominates. The final predicted state yielded a mean precipitate radius of 11.0 nm and a number density of 2.0 × 10²¹ m⁻³, indicating that the alloy achieved 60.2% of its theoretical precipitation progress.

By achieving >85% accuracy against experimental literature, this multi-scale framework provides a predictive roadmap for optimizing heat treatments—effectively bypassing expensive "trial-and-error" metallurgy to maximize both structural integrity and electrical conductivity.
