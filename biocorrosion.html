Biocorrosion of Titanium Alloys

the setup

An implant that corrodes doesn't just weaken — it leaches metal ions into the tissue around it. Titanium alloys are the default choice because of the thin oxide layer that forms on their surface, but that layer behaves differently depending on what's alloyed with the titanium. I wanted to actually compare three candidates instead of assuming one was fine.

Setting it up

Three alloys went into the comparison — Ti-6Al-4V, Ti-6Zr and Ti-8V — each modelled in COMSOL Multiphysics using a Tertiary Current Distribution interface (Nernst–Planck transport), coupled with Butler–Volmer kinetics at the electrode and a boundary ODE submodel for repassivation.

I pulled the differentiating parameters — i₀,bare, E_eq,bare, k_repass — straight out of each alloy's own .mph file rather than reusing textbook constants. Felt like the only honest way to compare them.

What came out of it

Ti-6Zr held up best of the three, on its comparative corrosion and repassivation behaviour — not the alloy I'd have bet on going in. The full comparison, with the governing equations and the alloy-by-alloy results, is written up as a proper academic report with sources cited.
