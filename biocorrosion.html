Simulation — COMSOL Multiphysics

Biocorrosion of Titanium Alloys

A comparative biocorrosion simulation of three titanium alloys, built to weigh their suitability for long-term implant service — where corrosion resistance matters as much as mechanical strength.

Alloys comparedTi-6Al-4V, Ti-6Zr, Ti-8V
Core physicsTertiary Current Distribution
KineticsButler–Volmer electrode model
DeliverableCited academic report

Method

Each alloy was modelled using a Tertiary Current Distribution interface (Nernst–Planck transport), coupled with Butler–Volmer kinetics at the electrode surface and a boundary ODE submodel governing repassivation. The differentiating parameters for each alloy — bare exchange current density (i₀,bare), bare equilibrium potential (E_eq,bare), and repassivation rate constant (k_repass) — were extracted directly from each alloy's own .mph archive, keeping the comparison grounded in alloy-specific data rather than generic literature values.

Why it matters

An implant that corrodes releases metal ions into surrounding tissue and loses mechanical integrity over years of service. Titanium alloys are the default choice for their passive oxide layer, but that layer behaves differently depending on the alloying elements — which is what this study set out to quantify.

Ti-6Zr was identified as the superior alloy of the three candidates, on the basis of its comparative corrosion and repassivation behaviour. The full comparison was compiled into a detailed academic report with inline citation of supporting literature.
