Biocorrosion of Titanium Alloys Under Disrupted Oxide Conditions

A comparative electrochemical assessment of Ti-6Zr and Ti-8V in simulated body fluid, referenced against the widely used Ti-6Al-4V baseline under mechanically disrupted (wear-corrosion) scenarios.

Alloys comparedTi-6Al-4V, Ti-6Zr, Ti-8V/Ti185
Test ScenarioTribocorrosion in SBF
Core MechanismDepassivation–Repassivation
DeliverableTechnical Supplement Report

The Tribocorrosion Paradigm

While all commercial titanium alloys form a self-healing TiO₂ passive film, in-service implants are subjected to micromotion and sliding contact (e.g., fretting at modular junctions). This mechanical action transiently fractures the oxide, exposing the bare metal to the biological fluid. This depassivation-repassivation cycle drives real-world implant degradation, causing sharp drops in open-circuit potential (OCP) and cumulative ion release.

Chemical & Microstructural Vulnerabilities

The baseline alloy, Ti-6Al-4V, forms an oxide containing Al₂O₃ and V₂O₅, both of which dissolve under physiological conditions. Once the oxide is breached, it releases toxic Al³⁺ and vanadate species. However, its α+β duplex structure provides some strain-hardening capacity to resist wear.

By contrast, Ti-8V (Ti185), a high-strength metastable β-titanium alloy, contains double the vanadium content. Its single-phase β microstructure lacks the strain-hardening capacity of Ti-6Al-4V. Analogue studies reveal that this causes significant wear-acceleration specifically in Simulated Body Fluid (SBF)—a wear-corrosion synergy not predicted by dry wear testing alone. This compounded with the larger reservoir of dissolvable V₂O₅ makes Ti-8V less favourable in disrupted-oxide scenarios.

The Superior Alternative: Ti-6Zr

Comparative analysis identifies Ti-6Zr as the strongest performer. Because Zirconium forms a chemically stable ZrO₂ oxide, the residual and reforming film is robust at every stage of the tribocorrosion cycle. Ti-6Zr exhibits a substantially smaller OCP collapse (~380 mV vs. 658 mV for the baseline), faster repassivation, and—critically—releases only biocompatible Ti⁴⁺ and Zr⁴⁺ ions.

Ti-6Zr decisively outperformed both the Ti-6Al-4V baseline and Ti-8V under disrupted-oxide conditions, demonstrating superior repassivation kinetics and a safe, non-toxic ion release profile.
