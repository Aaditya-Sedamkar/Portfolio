Biocorrosion of Titanium Alloys for Biomedical Implants

A comparative biocorrosion simulation of three titanium alloys — Ti-6Al-4V, Ti-6Zr and Ti-8V — built to weigh their suitability for long-term implant service, where corrosion resistance is as important as mechanical strength.

Alloys compared

Ti-6Al-4V, Ti-6Zr, Ti-8V

Core physics

Tertiary Current Distribution (Nernst–Planck)

Kinetics model

Butler–Volmer electrode kinetics

Deliverable

Academic report, cited

Why alloy choice matters here

An implant that corrodes releases metal ions into surrounding tissue and loses mechanical integrity over years of service. Titanium alloys are the default choice for their passive oxide layer, but that layer behaves differently depending on the alloying elements — which is what this study set out to quantify rather than assume.

Method

Each alloy was modelled using a Tertiary Current Distribution physics interface (Nernst–Planck transport), coupled with Butler–Volmer kinetics at the electrode surface and a boundary ODE submodel governing repassivation. Rather than relying on generic literature constants, the differentiating parameters for each alloy — the bare exchange current density (i₀,bare), the bare equilibrium potential (E_eq,bare), and the repassivation rate constant (k_repass) — were extracted directly from each alloy's own .mph archive, keeping the comparison grounded in alloy-specific electrochemical data.

Result

Ti-6Zr was identified as the superior alloy of the three candidates evaluated, on the basis of its comparative corrosion and repassivation behaviour.

The full comparison — model setup, governing equations, and alloy-by-alloy results — was compiled into a detailed academic report with inline citation of supporting literature.
